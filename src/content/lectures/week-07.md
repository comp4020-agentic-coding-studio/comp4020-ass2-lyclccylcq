Addresses the moment a subject becomes visibly self-conscious: body language,
weight distribution, asymmetry, what to do with hands and shoulders, and how
all of it changes across sitting, standing and walking.

Deliberately does not teach a list of fixed poses to strike. The emphasis is
on understanding why people become awkward in front of a lens, so a
photographer can respond to the person in front of them rather than reciting
a memorised pose.

## Themes for development

- why self-consciousness shows up physically, and what it looks like
- weight distribution and asymmetry as tools, not a checklist
- hands, shoulders, and the sitting / standing / walking difference
- explicitly avoiding a fixed-pose catalogue
