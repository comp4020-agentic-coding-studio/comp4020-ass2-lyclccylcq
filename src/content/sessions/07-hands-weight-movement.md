## Before the session

Bring a phone or camera with enough storage for short bursts. Learn how to
change exposure compensation and, where supported, shutter speed, aperture and
ISO. Choose one simple movement: walking between two marks, turning toward the
camera or moving hair with one clear action.

## In the session

### 1. Freeze the movement

Predict a useful shutter speed, make a short burst and inspect the eyes and face
at full size. Adjust only when the visible blur or timing requires it. Record
the setting and the best phase of the movement.

### 2. Compare depth

Make the same still portrait with a wider and smaller aperture. If the device
does not offer aperture control, change subject-to-background distance or
compare normal and portrait modes. Decide which version better supports the
place and person.

### 3. Create intentional blur

Make a sharp safety frame. Then use a slower shutter while either the subject
moves or the photographer moves the camera in one controlled direction. Keep
the result where the person remains recognisable enough for the chosen style.

### 4. Change the mood with exposure

Create a brighter and darker version using exposure compensation or manual
settings. Protect important facial detail and explain which version better
matches the intended feeling.

## Afterwards

Submit six frames: the sharp safety image, best frozen-action frame, two depth
versions, intentional blur and preferred exposure mood. Label the relevant
settings or phone method.

For each selected frame, complete the sentence: “I changed ______ because I
wanted ______ to look ______.” Marks reward visible intention, not manual mode
or technically impressive numbers.
