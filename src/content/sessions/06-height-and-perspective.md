## Before the session

Choose an ordinary campus or street location with at least three visible
distractions: people, signs, bins, cars, poles, bright patches or messy lines.
Bring a phone or camera with grid lines enabled. Do not choose a blank studio
wall; the exercise needs a background to solve.

## In the session

### 1. Make the default tourist frame

Photograph the person and location in the most obvious way. Keep this as the
baseline rather than deleting it.

### 2. Rescue the background by moving

Keep the subject in roughly the same area while the photographer moves left,
right, higher, lower, closer and farther. Identify which movement removes each
distraction.

### 3. Build four structures

Produce a centre composition, a rule-of-thirds frame, a negative-space frame
and one layered frame using foreground. At least one image must keep enough
environment to identify the place.

### 4. Scan the edges

Partners inspect each other's four frame edges, the outline around the head and
shoulders, and the brightest background object. Reshoot one frame after this
inspection.

## Afterwards

Submit the baseline tourist photograph and the four revised compositions.
Annotate what the photographer changed in position, distance or height and
which distraction it solved.

The subject chooses the frame where they are easiest to notice without losing
the feeling of the place. Include that reason with the contact sheet.
