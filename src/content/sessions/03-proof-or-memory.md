## Before the session

Bring the device you would realistically carry on a date: a phone, compact or
interchangeable-lens camera. Charge it, clear enough storage and bring a spare
battery or power bank if you already own one. Do not buy or borrow equipment
only to make your kit look more advanced.

Also bring the Week 2 date and shoot plan. Circle the most important photo
window and describe the intended result in three words—for example, “casual,
bright, environmental” or “soft, clean, romantic”.

## In the session

### 1. Device experience test

In small groups, make the same simple portrait with at least two available
devices or modes. This may be a phone and camera, phone main camera and
portrait mode, or modern phone and older compact. Compare:

- how quickly the device is ready;
- how the subject reacts to it;
- how it handles the current light;
- how much control it gives the photographer; and
- whether the result matches the intended style.

The task is not to declare one device the winner. Identify the situation where
each one becomes the sensible choice.

### 2. Focal-length and distance test

Make three portraits that approximate full-frame-equivalent 35mm, 50mm and
85mm views. Use suitable lenses where available; otherwise use the optical
cameras or focal-length options the device provides. Adjust camera distance to
make the subject a similar size in each frame.

Compare how each view changes:

- the amount of environment;
- the apparent relationship between foreground and background;
- the photographer's distance from the subject;
- ease of conversation and direction; and
- the style of portrait the frame suggests.

Do not use a wide lens very close to the face simply to match a tight crop.
The working distance is part of the test.

### 3. Aperture or separation test

If the camera allows aperture control, photograph the same frame once near the
widest useful aperture and once at a smaller aperture. If it does not, compare
the normal camera with portrait mode or change the subject-to-background
distance. Decide when blur helps the portrait and when it removes useful place
and story.

### 4. Accessory demonstration

The teaching team briefly demonstrates a spare battery and storage check,
direct flash, a small continuous light and a reflector. Students identify
which accessory could solve a real problem in their Week 2 plan and which ones
would only add weight.

## Afterwards

Submit a contact sheet containing:

1. the three focal-length views;
2. one device or aperture comparison; and
3. a short kit decision for the Week 2 plan.

Complete the sentence:

> I would bring ______ because this plan needs ______; I would leave ______ at
> home because ______.

The mark is based on the reasoning and the visible comparison, not on owning
the most expensive equipment.
