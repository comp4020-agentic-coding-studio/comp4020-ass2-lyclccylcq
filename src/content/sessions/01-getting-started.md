## Before the session

Bring three photographs from ordinary outings: one you think is technically
good, one your girlfriend or photographed partner likes, and one where your
opinions differ. Phone photographs are welcome. Ask permission before showing
another person's image to the group.

For each photograph, note what it was for: a private memory, a social post, a
profile image or a creative experiment.

## In the session

Begin by defining the minimum standard for a usable portrait. Check whether the
composition looks intentional, the person is clear and the selected moment is
usable. Then stop judging the photograph as if those checks settle the answer.

The photographer and subject independently select a favourite from the same
small group of images. Compare the reasons for each choice. Look for differences
in expression, angle, proportion, background, style, skin tone and intended use.

Next, examine four common breakdowns: failed composition, excessive editing,
fatigue after repeated attempts and a photo stop that disrupts the date. For
each case, identify the first decision that could have prevented the conflict.

## Afterwards

Write a short agreement for the next outing. Include the intended use of the
photographs, one visual preference, one editing boundary, when you will review
the first frames and a clear stopping condition. Keep it for comparison with
your Week 12 work.
