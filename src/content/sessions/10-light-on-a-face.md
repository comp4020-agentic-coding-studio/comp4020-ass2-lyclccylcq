## Before the session

Choose four simple actions from the lecture and write one destination, hand
task and gaze cue for each. Bring one ordinary object the subject can genuinely
use. Select three lines from the toxic photographer's bible and rewrite them in
your own words as respectful, actionable encouragement.

## In the session

### 1. Find the stiff baseline

Ask for one static portrait and notice where uncertainty appears in the hands,
shoulders, weight, gaze and expression. Do not criticise the subject; identify
which direction was missing.

### 2. Build a movement sequence

Photograph walking, turning, sitting or leaning. Give each movement a clear
start and destination. Add only one hand or gaze task at a time and use short
bursts to capture the transitions.

### 3. Practise the encouragement bible

After every sequence, state one specific success before changing anything.
When a frame fails, describe the photographic adjustment you will make instead
of assigning the failure to the subject.

### 4. Let the subject evaluate the process

The subject ranks each prompt for clarity, comfort and how recognisable the
result feels. Repeat only the strongest action with one small improvement.

## Afterwards

Submit an eight-frame contact sheet showing four actions and their transitions.
Include the exact direction and encouragement used for each pair.

Finish with two statements: one phrase you will never use again, and one
specific sentence that helped the subject feel successful.
