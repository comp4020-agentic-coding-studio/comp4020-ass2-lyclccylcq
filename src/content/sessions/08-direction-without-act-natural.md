## Before the session

Bring three visually compatible reference portraits. Your partner must also
like the general direction. Choose one that can be approached with available
locations, light and equipment; do not select a production whose essential
conditions cannot be recreated.

## In the session

### 1. Find

Select one reference and name its feeling in three specific words. Ask the
subject which part they want to preserve and which detail they do not want
copied.

### 2. Analyse

Complete a six-part breakdown:

- light direction and hardness;
- camera height, distance and view;
- person-to-background distance and composition;
- brightness and contrast;
- dominant colours; and
- action, expression or timing.

The observer challenges vague labels until each note describes a decision that
could guide a camera position or subject action.

### 3. Recreate

Choose the three most important decisions and reproduce them with the current
person and location. Make the closest useful version before applying any edit.
Show it to the subject and confirm that the style has not made them feel less
recognisable or comfortable.

### 4. Create one variation

Change one major decision—composition, amount of environment, movement, colour
relationship or camera position—while trying to preserve the same overall
feeling.

## Afterwards

Submit the reference, analysis, closest recreation and intentional variation.
Add a short style recipe covering feeling, light, camera, space, exposure,
colour and moment.

Finish by identifying which decision carried the style most strongly. “The
filter” is not accepted as the answer; point to a relationship visible before
post-production.
